# Showcase seeds (SEED-650)

The proof of concept needs worlds worth showing. `ichor_forge_cli showcase` forges a range of seeds, scores each one, ranks them and keeps the best few. The five kept are committed as `data/showcase_seeds.json` so the packaged build (SEED-644) can default to them.

    ichor_forge_cli showcase --seeds 1..200 --size 256 --top 5 --out showcase-out --json data/showcase_seeds.json

It writes `showcase-out/ranking.csv`, `showcase-out/ranking.md` (the ranked table plus the reasons of the top seeds) and `showcase-out/top/rank<k>_seed<N>.png`, the gallery cell of each top seed (hillshade tinted by biome, rivers in blue, the sanity caption). No window is opened and no GPU is used: the thumbnails are the same CPU layers `gallery` draws. Without `--out` only the markdown goes to stdout.

## Where the pieces live

- `forge-cli/include/forgecli/showcase.h` and `forge-cli/src/showcase.cpp`: the metrics, the scoring, the ranking and the tables. Pure libworldforge, no engine code.
- `forge-cli/walk/`: the engine side (`ichor_forgecli_walk`). It builds the heightfield, water and walking rules the game builds, finds the spawn with the game's `findForgedSpawn`, and walks the bot. The scoring takes it as a function, so the scoring tests need no engine.
- `kShowcaseWeights` in `showcase.h` is the one place the weights and targets live. The table below is a copy for reading: change the number in the header and here together.

## Scoring

Each term turns a measured number into permille (0 to 1000). Points are `weight * permille / 1000`, so a perfect world scores 100. All maths is integer, so a score is identical on every platform. No seed number appears anywhere in the scoring: every input is measured from the forged world, so the ranking follows whatever the forge produces on the tree it runs on.

| Term | Weight | Measured | Full marks | Falls to 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gates | 20 | the sanity gates (SEED-413 and the rest, SEED-500 land share, SEED-554 landmark coverage) on the first attempt, the seed itself with no reroll | every evaluated gate passes | any gate fails |
| land_share | 15 | share of cells above sea level | 25% to 65% | linear, 15 points outside the band |
| rivers | 10 | connected river networks of at least 10 cells | 12 | none |
| lakes | 5 | connected lakes of at least 4 cells | 4 | none |
| settlements | 10 | settlement count | 20 | none |
| tier_spread | 5 | distinct settlement tiers (hamlet, village, town, city) | 4 | none |
| walk_arrival | 15 | the walk bot, from the spawn to the nearest other settlement, arrives (SEED-492 walking rules) | arrives | blocked, timed out, or no settlement to walk to |
| biome_variety | 10 | distinct land biomes within 300 m of the spawn | 6 | none |
| spawn | 10 | the spawn is found and is not a pocket | open spawn | pocket or none |

Partial credit is linear: rivers 6 of 12 is 500 permille, and so on. The full marks are set high enough that a 256 world does not hit them all (a first draft with 3 rivers and 8 settlements gave 14 seeds the same 100.0), so the ranking is decided by the world and not by the seed number tie-break. Land share is the one term with a band: inside 25% to 65% it is full, and each permille outside costs 1000/150 permille of the term, so a world with 10% land or with 80% land scores 0 on it.

### The spawn, the walk and the pocket

The spawn is the one the game uses: `findForgedSpawn` over the settlements in world metres, on the roads phase terrain, so it is dry, gentle ground next to the settlement nearest the middle of the world.

A **pocket spawn** is a spawn from which the walker cannot get 100 m away on foot. The reach search steps over an 8 m lattice with the walking rules (steepness, deep water) and stops at 160 m. A pit ringed by cliffs reaches a few metres and is a pocket.

The **walk** goes to the nearest other settlement at least 60 m from the spawn that has dry, gentle ground within 120 m of its centre. The route is the engine's `findWalkRoute` (SEED-555), a search over the ground the walker itself assesses, followed by `walkTo` (the walk bot) with an arrival radius of 12 m. A world with no such settlement scores 0 on the walk term (nothing was shown to work). The bot gives an honest answer: arrived, or what stopped it.

### Reasons

Each term carries a note ("land 41.2%", "3 rivers", "walk arrived (412 m)"). The reasons of a seed list the terms that scored at least half first, then the ones that cost points, marked `weak:`, each with its points over its weight.

## Ranking

Highest total first, ties go to the lower seed. The ranked table for seeds 1 to 200 at size 256 is in the PR that last changed a default or a hash; it is not committed because it moves whenever the forge does. `data/showcase_seeds.json` is regenerated by running the command above on the tree you ship.

## `data/showcase_seeds.json`

An array of records (the data loader needs a string `id` on every record): `id`, `rank`, `seed`, `size`, `score` and `reasons`. The seeds are only valid for the forge they were ranked on: a PR that changes a default, a hash or the land share distribution (a phase, the config, the erosion) invalidates them, so re-run the command and commit the new file with that change.

## Tests

`engine/tests/test_forge_showcase.cpp` (scoring, ranking, tables, live worlds picked by search) and `engine/tests/test_forge_showcase_walk.cpp` (reach, route, probe, a full run into a temp directory). They check that scoring is deterministic, that a mostly ocean world scores below a balanced one, that zeroing a term changes the ranking, that the route gets round a ridge the straight walk cannot cross, and that a pit is a pocket.
